# Site History: Former Stelco Lands (Steelport)

## Overview

The former Stelco lands are located on the southern shore of Hamilton Harbour at 386 Wilcox Street in Hamilton, Ontario. For more than a century, the site served as a major centre of Canadian steel production and heavy industry.

Today, the property is being redeveloped as **Steelport**, an approximately 800-acre industrial district led by Slate Asset Management.

Understanding the site's industrial history, environmental legacy, and ongoing remediation efforts provides important context for evaluating future development opportunities.

---

## Historical Steel Production

Stelco's Hamilton Works operated as an integrated steel manufacturing facility.

At its peak, the site included:

* Coke production
* Blast furnace ironmaking
* Basic oxygen furnace steelmaking
* Continuous casting
* Rolling operations
* Finishing operations
* Galvanizing facilities

In 2010, Stelco reported an annual production capacity of approximately 2.5 million tonnes of steel.

Raw materials such as coal and iron ore arrived by ship through Hamilton Harbour. Coal was processed into coke, which was then used in blast furnace operations to produce iron. Molten iron was transferred to steelmaking facilities where it was refined into finished steel products.

---

## Industrial Infrastructure

The site's location was selected in part because of its transportation and industrial advantages.

Key infrastructure included:

* Deep-water harbour access
* Rail connections
* Highway access
* Industrial utility infrastructure
* Large industrial buildings and manufacturing facilities

The broader Steelport property contains approximately 3,400 metres of waterfront and direct access to the Great Lakes and St. Lawrence Seaway system.

These assets remain important components of the site's redevelopment potential.

---

## Waterfront Access

Hamilton Harbour played a central role in the site's operation.

The harbour enabled:

* Bulk shipment of raw materials
* Movement of finished steel products
* Industrial water intake
* Industrial water discharge following treatment

Water from Hamilton Harbour was used throughout the steelmaking process. According to Stelco's public reporting, process water was filtered and treated before leaving the facility.

The site's waterfront location continues to be one of its most valuable strategic assets.

---

## Environmental Legacy

The former Stelco lands are associated with more than a century of industrial activity.

Public records identify emissions and handling of substances including:

* Benzene
* Arsenic
* Cadmium
* Chromium
* Lead
* Mercury
* Manganese
* Zinc
* Polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs)
* Benzo[a]pyrene

Historically, emissions originated from multiple industrial processes, including coke ovens, blast furnaces, and steelmaking operations.

Over time, air quality regulations, monitoring programs, and pollution control equipment contributed to reductions in several emissions.

The environmental legacy of the site extends beyond the property itself and forms part of the broader industrial history of Hamilton Harbour.

---

## Hamilton Harbour and Randle Reef

Hamilton Harbour has undergone significant environmental improvement efforts over the past several decades.

One of the most significant projects is the remediation of Randle Reef, located in the southwest corner of Hamilton Harbour.

According to the Government of Canada, Randle Reef is the largest contaminated sediment site on the Canadian side of the Great Lakes.

The site contains approximately:

* 615,000 cubic metres of contaminated sediment
* 60 hectares of affected area

Contamination is linked to industrial activity dating back to the nineteenth century.

The Randle Reef remediation project began in 2015 and includes:

### Stage 1

Construction of an engineered containment facility.

### Stage 2

Dredging and placement of contaminated sediment into the containment facility, followed by capping of surrounding contaminated areas.

### Stage 3

Final habitat restoration and project completion.

Federal sources indicate completion is currently scheduled for 2027.

Stelco participated as one of the funding partners supporting the project.

---

## Redevelopment as Steelport

In 2022, Slate Asset Management acquired approximately 800 acres of industrial land and buildings from Stelco.

The redevelopment vision for Steelport includes:

* Advanced manufacturing
* Research facilities
* Industrial innovation
* Energy infrastructure
* AI infrastructure opportunities
* Long-term economic development

The site represents one of the largest industrial redevelopment opportunities in Canada.

---

## Relevance to TEEHC

The former Stelco lands present a unique case study because they combine:

* Existing industrial infrastructure
* Waterfront access
* A documented environmental legacy
* Active harbour restoration efforts
* Emerging AI and industrial redevelopment plans

This creates an opportunity to explore a broader question:

**Can future computing and industrial infrastructure contribute positively to the communities and ecosystems that support them?**

At present, this remains a question for investigation rather than a proven outcome.

The Steelport redevelopment provides a real-world context in which stewardship-oriented infrastructure concepts can be examined against practical, economic, environmental, and community considerations.
