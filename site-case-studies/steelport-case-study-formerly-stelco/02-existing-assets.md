# Existing Assets: Former Stelco Lands / Steelport

## Overview

The former Stelco lands already hold several assets that make the site strategically significant.

These assets do not resolve the environmental, energy, water, or community concerns connected to future AI infrastructure.

They do explain why the site is being positioned as a major industrial redevelopment opportunity.

Steelport begins from an existing industrial footprint, with waterfront access, transportation infrastructure, development scale, energy planning, and proximity to Hamilton’s broader industrial and research ecosystem.

## Land Scale

The Steelport site covers approximately 800 acres on Hamilton’s industrial waterfront.

Public materials describe it as one of the largest industrial redevelopment opportunities in Canada.

The site’s scale allows for phased development, large industrial parcels, public realm planning, energy infrastructure, stormwater systems, and future employment uses.

The draft subdivision plan has been publicly reported to include:

* 684 acres of gross development area
* 62 acres of public right-of-way
* 91 acres of open space and amenity blocks
* 23 acres of water bodies
* 78 acres retained by Stelco Steel Mills for ongoing operations

This scale matters because Steelport is being planned as a district, rather than a single building or isolated facility.

## Waterfront Access

Steelport has approximately 3,400 metres of waterfront along Hamilton Harbour.

The waterfront is one of the site’s most important assets.

It provides access to the Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence Seaway system, and it creates opportunities for port activity, industrial logistics, waterfront restoration, public access, and potential water-based cooling studies.

The harbour location also carries responsibility.

Hamilton Harbour has already undergone decades of environmental recovery work. Any new industrial development on this shoreline should be evaluated in relation to that recovery.

## Port and Seaway Access

Steelport is positioned within the historic port of Hamilton.

Public materials describe the site as having direct access to the Great Lakes and the St. Lawrence Seaway.

This gives the site strategic value for industries that depend on large-scale movement of goods, equipment, raw materials, or manufactured products.

For future AI and advanced manufacturing infrastructure, this port access may also support movement of large equipment, modular building components, electrical infrastructure, cooling systems, and construction materials.

## Rail, Road, and Airport Connectivity

Steelport is publicly described as connecting rail, road, and water.

Slate has identified on-site rail, Ontario highway access, proximity to the U.S. land border, and nearby airports in Hamilton and Toronto as part of the site’s strategic location.

This transportation network is a major asset.

It supports industrial logistics, construction staging, workforce access, supply chain movement, and future tenant flexibility.

Hamilton’s airport is also a significant cargo airport, which adds another layer of logistics value.

## Existing Industrial Footprint

The site is already heavily industrialized.

This is one of its most important planning advantages.

The former Stelco lands contain existing industrial land, buildings, infrastructure corridors, utility systems, and a long-standing industrial context.

A redevelopment of this kind can use an existing industrial footprint rather than placing major new infrastructure on untouched land.

That does not remove the need for environmental care.

It does make the site more appropriate for industrial reuse than many greenfield alternatives.

## Development Capacity

Public materials state that the site’s current zoning allows for up to approximately 12 million square feet of development.

This makes Steelport a large-scale industrial pipeline, with enough capacity to accommodate multiple uses over time.

Potential uses identified publicly include:

* AI infrastructure
* Advanced manufacturing
* Logistics
* Production facilities
* Research facilities
* Clean infrastructure
* Employment-supporting uses

This creates flexibility.

It also creates a need for clear environmental performance expectations across the entire district.

## Energy Planning Assets

Steelport is being positioned as power-ready and supported by high-capacity infrastructure.

Public Steelport materials refer to existing high-capacity sustainable infrastructure, onsite power management, district energy, industrial-scale bay water cooling, and energy resiliency.

Hamilton Community Enterprises is publicly identified as a partner in the site’s clean infrastructure planning.

The City of Hamilton’s connected community strategy states that HCE is collaborating with Slate on an integrated energy strategy for the Stelco port lands, including:

* A micro-grid
* District energy
* Distributed energy resources
* Energy storage systems
* Waste heat repurposing for the City’s District Energy System

This is one of the strongest existing assets from a TEEHC perspective.

The site is already being discussed in relation to district-scale energy systems, rather than only conventional utility service.

## District Energy Context

Hamilton already has district energy experience.

Hamilton Community Enterprises has operated a downtown district energy system since 2002, serving institutional, commercial, and residential buildings.

HCE has also completed an Energy Harvesting Study examining the technical feasibility and commercial viability of using residual thermal energy from Hamilton’s Bayfront Industrial Area to heat buildings.

Public materials describe the proposed thermal corridor as technically feasible and commercially viable, with potential greenhouse gas reductions and recycled heat delivery.

This matters because heat recovery is often discussed as an abstract possibility in data centre planning.

In Hamilton, there is already local institutional experience examining industrial waste heat as a usable energy source.

## Water and Cooling Context

Steelport’s waterfront location creates potential for water-based cooling strategies.

Public materials refer to industrial-scale bay water cooling.

This should be treated carefully.

Hamilton Harbour itself is likely too shallow for true deep lake cooling in the same sense as Toronto’s system. However, the site’s proximity to Lake Ontario and the Burlington Ship Canal makes deeper cooling strategies worth further technical study.

At this stage, the defensible statement is that the site has waterfront cooling potential.

Site-specific engineering, environmental, thermal, intake, discharge, water quality, and permitting studies would be required before stronger claims could be made.

## Open Space, Stormwater, and Public Realm Assets

Steelport’s draft subdivision materials include substantial open space and public realm elements.

Publicly reported elements include:

* Public right-of-way
* Open space and amenity blocks
* Water bodies
* Trails and pathways
* Active transportation corridors
* A proposed four-kilometre Steelport Loop trail
* A proposed Lagoonscape
* Waterfront access areas
* The Pipe Gallery
* The Battery
* The Waterfront

The Lagoonscape has been described as a regenerated biodiverse landscape that also functions as stormwater management infrastructure.

These elements are important because they create possible locations for ecological restoration, public education, habitat design, stormwater treatment, and long-term environmental monitoring.

## Public Access Potential

Parts of the Hamilton Harbour industrial waterfront have been inaccessible to the public for generations.

Steelport materials describe a plan to open new areas of the historic harbour through waterfront access, trails, public spaces, and community hubs.

Public access is an asset only if it is designed carefully.

For this case study, the important point is that the site plan already includes public realm ambitions. Those ambitions could support community benefit, environmental education, and visible stewardship if they are carried through with care.

## Workforce and Institutional Context

Hamilton has a strong industrial workforce history and a growing post-secondary ecosystem.

McMaster University and Mohawk College are both located in Hamilton.

Mohawk College is publicly described as one of Ontario’s largest apprenticeship trainers, with 2,400 apprenticeship students annually.

Public materials also identify Hamilton’s post-secondary institutions as part of the region’s skilled workforce and talent pipeline.

This creates potential for future partnerships in:

* Skilled trades
* Building systems
* Environmental monitoring
* Engineering
* Data infrastructure
* Applied research
* Clean technology
* Workforce training

For TEEHC, this is relevant because a restorative infrastructure model would need local technical capacity, not only capital investment.

## Adjacent Industrial Ecosystem

Steelport sits within Hamilton’s Bayfront Industrial Area.

This places it near existing industrial operators, port infrastructure, energy users, potential heat sources, and potential heat customers.

The surrounding industrial context creates both challenges and opportunities.

It may increase cumulative environmental pressure.

It may also create opportunities for shared infrastructure, heat recovery, district energy, workforce training, industrial symbiosis, and coordinated environmental monitoring.

This should be studied carefully.

The asset is the concentration of industrial activity.

The responsibility is managing cumulative impact.

## Economic Development Momentum

Public materials describe Steelport as a major long-term economic development project.

Earlier public estimates referenced up to 23,000 jobs across the Greater Toronto and Hamilton Area and up to $3.8 billion in economic impact over the next decade.

More recent Steelport materials reference larger construction-related job and GDP estimates.

These figures should be treated as projections from public materials, not confirmed outcomes.

They are useful for understanding the scale of ambition behind the project.

They should not be presented as evidence that the project will deliver those results.

## Existing Environmental Context

The environmental legacy of the former Stelco lands and Hamilton Harbour is part of the site’s existing condition.

This is not an asset in the ordinary sense.

It is a defining baseline.

The site’s history, the harbour’s recovery work, and nearby remediation efforts create a serious responsibility for future development.

They also create an opportunity.

If Steelport is developed with strong environmental performance, transparent reporting, and measurable restoration, the project could contribute to the next phase of Hamilton Harbour’s recovery story.

That remains a possibility for investigation.

It is not yet a demonstrated outcome.

## Working Conclusion

Steelport’s existing assets are substantial.

The site offers scale, waterfront access, port and seaway connectivity, rail and road infrastructure, industrial zoning, redevelopment capacity, energy planning potential, district energy context, public realm ambitions, and proximity to Hamilton’s workforce and research ecosystem.

These assets make the site unusually well positioned for a serious case study.

They also raise the standard.

A site with this much industrial history, public visibility, and redevelopment potential should be evaluated against clear expectations for water, energy, heat recovery, ecological restoration, public transparency, and community benefit.
